# Balanced development update

This is exploratory development evidence, not part of the independent 510-run primary-plus-replication cohort. Six underlying parents, one per benchmark family and two at each customer size, were paired on original versus combined. Four component ablations used a prespecified three-parent subset. All five methods used the same scenarios, fleet, physics, objective and five-second decision limit.

All 120 saved raw records passed independent physical replay and source/scenario/hash checks. The combined variant yielded +3 served customers across 30 paired method-runs (4 gains, 1 loss; 1 harmed parents). The frozen selection rule requires no aggregate service loss, no repeated family harm, and a useful service or conditional-quality gain. The stricter gate requiring positive aggregate service, at least two improving parents, no harmed parent, and no method-level aggregate service loss is post hoc. A single ACS loss is not, by itself, a demonstrated failure of the frozen rule. No enhanced promotion is justified by this exploratory evidence; historical regressions remain disqualifying for deployment. No variant is retroactively promoted and no enhanced independent holdout was opened.

Cumulative charged wall time: 2.051 h of the six-hour development allowance; attempted-worker CPU time: 2.021 h, summed over all original and technical-correction supervisor ledger entries, including failures and process overhead. One original whole-worker timeout was preserved and the remaining eight jobs used a separately frozen 900-second technical correction. The five-second MPC decision budget did not change.

A separate two-scenario/two-method technical probe found 1.50x sequential-to-two-worker throughput with identical served counts. This is a small shared-machine engineering result, not controlled paper timing. See the [probe report](../two_worker_probe_v1/RESULTS.md).
